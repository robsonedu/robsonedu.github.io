How to Use the Colors Personality Assessment

Written by Allen Robson, District Instructional Coach and secondary ELA teacher

The Colors Personality Assessment is a free personality quiz for students. Each student ranks four statements for eleven questions and finds their colors: Orange, Green, Blue and Gold. Everyone has some of each color. Their top colors show how they like to work, learn and connect with others.

How I use it in my classroom

Using personality has more impact than traditional behavior metrics like talking and distractions. Personality takes into account the strengths and weaknesses of all the personality types. For example, GREEN thinks deeply about concepts, but tends to overthink at times. BLUE is a great listener, but tends to avoid conflict. GOLD has some leadership skills, but can come across as bossy. ORANGE can bring energy, but can be easily distracted.

I usually give the personality test early in the year, and when new students arrive. It can be done in about 5 minutes. I like to have students take it and upload a screenshot to an LMS. That way I always have that data on hand. The assessment works great for 6th–12th grade and above. I've even used it with whole school staff and PLCs. Students consistently agree with the results, but they can quickly retake it at any time and report their results to you.

I use the results to strategically group students. For example, in a 4-person team, 3–4 GREENS will get stuck on something by overthinking it. BLUES care so much about others' feelings that no one can make a decision. Too many GOLDS in a group could have conflict because everyone wants to lead with their vision. Several ORANGES might have trouble finishing because they got distracted. While it's generally okay to have 2 of the same color sometimes, a 4-person group made up of one of each color works in great synergy.

Get started in four steps

  1. Take it yourself first. It takes about five minutes, and you'll know what students will see.
  2. Share the link. Copy the student link or show the QR code. Students type their first name and start.
  3. Students rank each set. For each question they rank four statements from most like me to least like me. There are no wrong answers.
  4. Collect the results. Students save their result card as an image or email it, so you have everyone's colors.

The four colors

OrangeWitty, bold and spontaneous. Loves variety, action and freedom. The energizer.
GreenAnalytical, logical and curious. Values competence and ideas. The analyst.
BlueWarm, empathetic and idealistic. Values harmony and relationships. The supporter.
GoldDependable, organized and responsible. Values structure and tradition. The organizer.

Teacher questions

How long does it take?

About five minutes. There are eleven questions, and each one asks students to rank four statements.

What grades is it for?

Works great for grades 6–12 and older. I've even seen it work with students as young as 4th grade.

Do students need an account?

No. There's no sign-in, and answers stay on each student's device. Nothing is sent to RobsonEDU.

How do students share their results with me?

At the end, students can save their result card as an image or email it to you. You can also have them hold up their screen or write their colors on a sticky note.

What if a student runs out of time?

After five questions, the teacher tools can show results right away, so a student who runs out of time still gets their colors.

Can I give it on paper?

Yes. Use the printable version and have students rank the statements with a pencil.

How do I use the colors to make groups?

Add each student's top color as a column in your class roster in Grouping Studio, then balance your groups by it. Each group gets a mix of personalities: an energizer, an analyst, a supporter and an organizer.
