TeamCards

TeamCards give every student a clear number, partner, and role right on the table — so you can randomly call on students, check for understanding, and assign jobs like who starts, who shares, or who collects materials. Each file includes several colors: use one per team, match your décor, or go multi for an extra layer of strategic grouping. TeamCards work best on tables; for individual seats, try labels or stickers instead.

How to Use TeamCard Table Mats

Written by Allen Robson, District Instructional Coach and former secondary ELA teacher

TeamCard Table Mats are free printable mats for cooperative learning. Lay one in the middle of each team's table and every student has a number, a partner and a face partner, so you can call on students, pair them up and run team structures without a word of setup. Sizes for teams of 3, 4, 5 and 6, each in eight or nine colors. They're easy to pick up and put down, or you can fix them to the table with tape or Velcro.

How I use it in my classroom

I use TeamCards to explicitly organize how students interact. Each student gets a seat number (1–4) and a partner letter, A or B (C in some cases). I put a different color on each team table so that I have a red team, blue team, green team, etc. I've also used one color for all tables and numbered them in the center, or used team names like Lions, Tigers and Bears, written on or added with stickers. The possibilities are really endless. Depending on your class layout, you could even use labels, stickers or dots to recreate this organization with your own flair!

I use them any time I want students to interact with me or each other. For example, when students were reading a text, I would say "1s, you start with paragraph 1, then 2s take the next, and so on until you've finished the piece." After that I might say, "Now summarize what you just read with your face partner; A's summarize paragraphs 1–2, B's summarize paragraphs 3–4." Lastly, to check for understanding, I would randomly select one person from the team using a number selector or cards, "3s, stand up and be ready to share for your team!" Then we'd celebrate the 3s by clapping, and I'd ask each one to share their understanding of the text per our purpose or learning target. There are so many ways to use the TeamCard system. They're great for Think-Pair-Share, cooperative learning, Kagan specific structures like RoundRobin, Numbered Heads Together, and much more.

I use TeamCards with the Numbers tool in our SELECTr PLUS selector. It's set to no repeats, so no one gets called on unfairly, and you can turn that on or off. Add in some novelty by mixing it up using name selectors like the wheel or drum for a change of pace or if you only need to hear from a few instead of one from each team. Pair TeamCards with Grouping Studio to take your strategic grouping and teaming system to the next level! Use TIMEr PLUS to time your student interactions for maximum efficiency.

Get started in four steps

  1. Pick your team size. Teams of 4 are the sweet spot. Use 3 or 5 for a remainder team, and 6 for larger tables.
  2. Print and place. Open the PDF, print it full page and set one mat in the center of each team's table. Laminate them if you can, so they last all year.
  3. Seat students by number. Each student sits at a number. Shoulder partners sit side by side; face partners sit across.
  4. Call it out. "Number 3s, share your team's answer." "Shoulder partners, compare your work." Or spin Numbers in SELECTr PLUS to pick a seat.

Teacher questions

Which file should I print?

The main card on each size. It prints as full pages, ready to use. The crop-mark version is only for trimming the mats down with a paper cutter; it has thin cutting lines around the edges.

Should I laminate them?

I print them in color on cardstock and laminate them, and they last all year. You could also put them in sheet protector sleeves. Other times, I have printed them in plain black and white with no lamination for more temporary uses. Students will likely need reminders to leave them alone, which helps them last longer.

What do I do with an odd number of students?

Use TeamCard 4 for most teams, and a TeamCard 3 or 5 for the one team that's left over. Every student still has a number and a partner.

How do I introduce TeamCards to students?

I have them on the table the first day of school. I tell students explicitly to note which number seat they're in, who their face and shoulder partners are, and whether they are an A or B (or C). They practice with each several times a class and throughout the week. I start with numbers to get the team talking to one another. Then I choose one from each team to share with the class. The next time, I have them interact with face or shoulder partners. Then another class share out. I want them to get very comfortable with the system, so when I say who goes first or who they're working with, students know automatically how to interact, without having to figure it out or ask me. It's right there in front of them. When I work with other groups in other classrooms, I treat it like it's the first day and briefly explain the system.

How do I call on students with TeamCards?

Call a number after the team has talked it over: "Number 2s, stand and share." Every team is accountable, and no one is singled out. To make it random, spin Numbers in SELECTr PLUS.

Do the colors mean anything?

I use a different color on each team table so that I have a red team, blue team, green team, etc. It reduces confusion: if every team had the same color, team numbers could get mixed up with seat numbers. I also use the multicolored card in some cases, because I mentally attach a color to an ability level: for example, blue is high and green is medium-high. That works well when I have an intervention group and I have data, but I don't know the students' ability in my head and I'm with them for a short time. I can easily tell their level by which seat they're in. It helps with my in-the-moment instructional decisions.
